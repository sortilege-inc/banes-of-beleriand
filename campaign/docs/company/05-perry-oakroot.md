A Man of Bree and one of the gate-wardens of Bree-land. He spent many cold, lonely nights on watch at
the gates with only the stars and the owls for company, and he knows a good many travellers by sight;
some of them he has come to know as good folk, not at all like Bill Ferny and his cronies. He had not
been free from fear or care for some time when [[03-lily-goatleaf|Lily]], his distant cousin, set out
after the Ranger, and he went with her: not to bring her back, but to help.

He carries the maps that came down to him from his grandfather Herbert, marked now by
[[tom-bombadil|Tom]] in pencil, smudged with jam, and with Perry's own copy of the flower map from
the [[mathom-house|Mathom-house]] taped to one edge. He is the Company's guide. He remembered the man
in the blue-and-white belt from the gate at Bree, the man his superior had waved through.

He is no archer and no healer, and he knows it; he sat with [[moriel|Moriel]] and learned what he
could about wounds. He is a friend to dogs, to card-players, and to anyone lonely. At the Havens he
cured himself of his fear of the sea with the crab-catcher [[otto|Otto]], and sat on the walls with
the old guard [[mablin|Mablin]]. The broken Númenórean sword from the ruins was reforged for him as a
long-hafted axe. It was Perry who proposed the beacon-fires between the places of the Banes.

In the valley below the Blue Mountains he saw war for the first time, and kin turned on kin. It took
something from him. He came out of it among the crumbs of Etta's pastries, ready, and hardened, and
having lost his innocence ([[09-the-blue-mountains|The Blue Mountains]]).
